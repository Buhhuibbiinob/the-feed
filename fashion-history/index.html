1947 · silhouette · Paris

The New Look was a fabric argument before it was a shape

filed by amaiya · 12 minutes ago

plate 01
Bar suit, 1947

Dior's Bar jacket takes about fifteen metres of cloth for a skirt that a 1944 ration board would have refused outright. Read the hip padding and the boned waist tape as the point rather than the decoration: the silhouette is built from the inside, by a corsetière, and only then draped. Every telling of the New Look that starts at the hemline starts one layer too late.

1962–1975 · subculture · Kingston

Rude boy tailoring, and what a short trouser actually signalled

filed by m.okonjo · yesterday

plate 02
Kingston, c. 1966

The cropped leg gets written up as a mod import. It wasn't. Jamaican tailors were cutting above the ankle because the shoe was the expensive part and the shoe had to be seen. The suit travelled to Brixton with the music, the ankle stayed, and by 1979 two-tone had it back on record sleeves with the reason for it filed off.

1989 · construction · Antwerp

The white basting stitch, left in on purpose

filed by studio_b · 2 days ago

plate 03
Exposed basting

A basting stitch is temporary by definition — it holds cloth while the real seam is made, then it is pulled. Leaving it visible turns the garment into a document of its own making. Margiela's four white tacks at the shoulder do the same job as a signature and a receipt at once: this was assembled, by someone, at a moment you can see.

1920s · cloth & dye · Kano

Indigo pits, and the blue that rubs off deliberately

filed by amaiya · 4 days ago

plate 04
Kofar Mata dye pits

Fermentation-vat indigo builds colour by oxidation, one dip at a time, so depth is a record of labour and nothing else. The surface bloom that transfers to skin — the thing a modern finisher would call a fault and wash out — was the proof of dip count. A whole grammar of value, thrown away the moment colourfastness became the standard.

1973 · houses · Versailles

The Battle of Versailles, counted properly

filed by r.velez · 5 days ago

plate 05
Grand Divertissement

Five American designers against five French houses, and the account that survives is about Halston and Saint Laurent. The thing that actually moved was the walk: ten Black models working to music, no sets to speak of, against a French production with a Cinderella carriage. What won was rhythm as staging, and the runway has been paying royalties on it ever since.

1996–2004 · patterns · Tokyo

Flat pattern as a moral position

filed by studio_b · last week

plate 06
Two-dimensional cut

Draping starts from a body and subtracts. Flat pattern starts from a rectangle of cloth and refuses to waste it. Everything that follows — the armhole that sits low, the sleeve that reads as a fold, the garment that looks wrong on a hanger and right on a person — comes out of that one decision about the offcut.
